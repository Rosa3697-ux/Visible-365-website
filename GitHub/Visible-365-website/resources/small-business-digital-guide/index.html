Small business digital starter guide

Start with a domain you own, a professional identity and a clear customer contact process.

1. Own your domain and business email

Register the domain in your name or business entity. Enable MFA, record renewal dates and confirm you can transfer your domain. Set up a professional inbox and test sending and receiving.

2. Give customers a clear reason to contact you

Choose three core services and describe who they are for, where you operate and what the next step is. A homepage, service page and contact page are a useful starting point.

3. Use a consistent brand

Choose a readable logo, a small color palette and a consistent name. Use real images with permission, and avoid copied reviews or logos that suggest partnerships you do not have.

4. Get discoverable responsibly

Make page titles descriptive, link to your services, publish accurate contact information and submit your sitemap in Search Console. A Google Business Profile is only appropriate if the business meets eligibility rules.

5. Compare website costs and responsibilities

Compare setup, recurring management, domain, email, changes, cancellation and export rights. Use the cost calculator to compare the same period and scope; cheaper is not automatically better.

6. Measure what matters

Track actual inquiries, response time and booked projects rather than likes alone. Review monthly and make changes based on what customers ask.
